Stakeholders, elicitation, and scope

If the client asks for a feature, is that the requirement?

Today, an answer must change the frame.

Textbook study

§4.3, pp. 112-116. Printed pages in Sommerville (2016), 10th edition, Global Edition. See the session 3 reading guide for the study question and optional selections.

Session question

A feature request is evidence of a need, not automatically a settled requirement. We will trace one question through to a project decision.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

One answer must change the frame.

Say · rehearsal script

Last week we considered how to organise work under uncertainty. Today we will investigate one of those uncertainties. A client can ask confidently for a feature while we still do not understand the outcome they need. Our job is to make that gap visible. One answer must change the frame.

You will preserve a question before receiving an answer, then show what the answer changes. Keep the original wording, because the difference will be the evidence of your reasoning. Keep the evidence visible. [Pause for thinking, responses, or the stated work time before moving on.]

Explanation8 min

Two overlapping groups

User

Interacts directly with the system.

Stakeholder

Is affected by it or can shape its constraints and success.

A person can be both. Some important stakeholders are not users.

Useful distinction

The labels help us notice missing perspectives. They are not a hierarchy of whose needs automatically win.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

Look beyond the person using the screen.

Say · rehearsal script

A user directly interacts with the service. A stakeholder may be affected by it or shape its constraints without opening the interface. The organiser might be both; accessibility support can contribute important knowledge without booking a place. Look beyond the person using the screen.

These labels help us notice missing perspectives. They do not decide whose view wins. Which affected person would be easiest to miss if we only interviewed the student at the screen? Keep the evidence visible. [Pause for thinking, responses, or the stated work time before moving on.]

Why separate them

A feature may address a real need, but it can also embed an unsupported solution or ignore a constraint.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

Separate the outcome from the proposed response.

Say · rehearsal script

A need describes an outcome or problem. A reminder is a proposed response. A fixed event date may constrain our response, while believing everyone reads email is an assumption we still need to examine. Separate the outcome from the proposed response.

We do not have to reject a useful feature idea. We need to understand what it is intended to achieve and which parts of our interpretation remain unconfirmed. Use these distinctions to annotate the statements. Keep the evidence visible. [Pause for thinking, responses, or the stated work time before moving on.]

Explanation10 min

A consequential question

Ask for evidence that could change a decision

Target

One known uncertainty.

Answerable

A stakeholder can respond from their knowledge.

Consequence

The answer could alter scope, priority, or wording.

Question test

A broad invitation can start a conversation, but today’s question must support an inspectable reasoning chain.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

Ask something that could change a decision.

Say · rehearsal script

A useful question targets an uncertainty, goes to someone who could answer it, and has a consequence for our frame. Asking whether someone likes an idea often tells us less than asking what happens in the situation we are trying to improve. Ask something that could change a decision.

Try this check: imagine two plausible answers. Would they lead you to different wording, scope, or priorities? If both leave your decision untouched, sharpen the question before asking it. Keep the evidence visible. [Pause for thinking, responses, or the stated work time before moving on.]

Anchor · if you lose the thread

Explanation10 min

The answer can move different parts

Change

Objective, scope boundary, requirement direction, priority, or assumption.

Preserve

A conflict, dependency, ambiguity, or unanswered question.

No forced certainty

A strong frame uses what is known and makes consequential uncertainty visible. It does not pretend one interview completes elicitation.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

A useful revision need not add a feature.

Say · rehearsal script

An answer can justify doing less, sequencing something later, or setting a condition before committing. It can also support a new requirement, provided we can trace the need and the scope. A useful revision need not add a feature.

Keep the remaining conflict explicit. One stakeholder cannot settle another person’s access needs or decide a delivery estimate on their behalf. We have improved the frame without completing every conversation. Keep the evidence visible. [Pause for thinking, responses, or the stated work time before moving on.]

Delivery guidance · not spoken

Contrast

Boundary

Do not announce one required revision unless the prepared answer makes alternatives indefensible.
