Organise the next delivery

Use only the supplied context. The observations do not establish the technical cause of the booking problems.

What happened

Campus Event lets students browse events, reserve or cancel places, and receive reminders. Organisers need trustworthy capacity and attendance information. A polished demonstration showed a successful reservation. After launch, students reported duplicate reservations and conflicting success messages for the last place.

The original delivery plan

Requirements and screen approval came first. Separate booking and notification work followed, with integration near the end. Organisers saw the complete reservation journey only at the final demonstration.

The demonstration did not explore competing bookings or an unclear response. The sponsor approved launch. The plan did not name who would bring unresolved booking concerns to that decision.

02

Conditions for the next delivery

  • The next advertised event opens for booking in four delivery weeks. Its date is fixed. The sponsor can reduce the software scope or postpone online booking, but must agree an alternative with the organiser before invitations go out at the end of delivery week three.
  • The same small delivery team handles booking, organiser information, and notifications. There is no additional team. These parts must work together before real students rely on a booking outcome.
  • Reliable reservation, cancellation, and capacity information take priority. The organiser can send reminders manually for this event if automated reminders are deferred.
  • An organiser and two student volunteers can review a short example or trial journey at the end of each delivery week. They cannot join daily meetings. Their feedback can identify misunderstandings; it cannot establish reliability for every user or condition.
  • The delivery lead chooses the work order and requests checks. The organiser agrees the event's operating rules. The sponsor decides scope and whether online booking opens. Support can report confusing outcomes and escalate concerns to the delivery lead.
  • The team has not established how much work is needed to correct the booking behaviour. Do not assume the faults have been fixed or that the available time is sufficient.

Campus Event · Part B · controlled change

04

One new request

Sponsor request

When an event is full, students should be able to join a waiting list and receive an offer if someone cancels.

The organiser wants fewer empty places but has not agreed how long an offer should remain open. Students have not tried a waiting-list journey. The team has not estimated the work.

A waiting-list offer depends on knowing that a place has actually become available. The team must also distinguish an offer from a confirmed reservation.

The four-week window and team capacity remain unchanged. The sponsor will consider deferring the waiting list if the recommendation explains the consequence. Do not add payments, external systems, or other features.

Your recommendation must show

  1. an approach or combination and how Part B affects the provisional choice;
  2. three to five activities or decisions in dependency order;
  3. one feedback point before a commitment (a decision that becomes expensive to reverse), including what decision can still change;
  4. one plausible rejected alternative for a contextual reason;
  5. one remaining risk, an owner, and an action or escalation.

You may defer, explore before deciding, or propose a bounded delivery. Label assumptions; do not invent feasibility evidence.

Historical lens

In 1970, Winston Royce warned that discovering practical limitations only during late testing could force substantial redesign. His response included early modelling and customer involvement alongside planned stages and documentation.

Source: W. W. Royce, Managing the Development of Large Software Systems (1970), reprint pp. 329, 334, 337.
