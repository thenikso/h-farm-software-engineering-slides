A test makes a bounded claim

Pass means: this check found no failure here

Requirement

What behaviour or quality matters?

Risk

What failure would matter most?

Test

What observation could challenge our confidence?

Limit

What remains outside this result?

Review note

Useful test reasoning connects the check to a requirement or risk and states the limit of the result.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

Connect requirement, risk, check, and limit.

Say · rehearsal script

A requirement tells us what matters; a risk tells us why failure matters. A test arranges a condition and checks an observation. If I reserve the last place, the stored count should respect the capacity. That is a useful check because people rely on the booking outcome.

Even a correctly passing check has a boundary. The next test should answer a consequential open question, rather than merely increase the test count. [Point from requirement to limit and ask which missing condition would change confidence.]

Delivery guidance · not spoken

Transition

Point across the four cards. “We choose a test because a failure matters, not because a taxonomy asks for it.”

Listen for

Rescue or cut

Do not expand into a catalogue of test levels. The protected decision is which test adds the most useful confidence.

Explanation12 min

Passing sequential tests

Order can hide interaction

Two actions may each pass alone while failing when their timing overlaps.

Sequential evidence

One action completes before the next begins.

Interaction risk

A second action can observe or change shared state at the wrong moment.

Honest conclusion

The sequential result remains useful, but it does not exercise overlap.

Review note

A test can be correctly implemented and still omit a consequential condition.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

Sequential success does not exercise overlap.

Say · rehearsal script

The second test waits until the first reservation has finished before trying the next. That is sequential evidence. The test matters: it checks that a later request is refused once capacity has been reached.

Now consider a different condition: two requests are in progress together. Both may make a decision before either has completed. Our three tests do not arrange that timing. This identifies a gap in the evidence; it does not establish what every deployment will do. [Draw two timelines with overlapping decision points, without describing a fix.]

We can keep the passing result and narrow the claim. The useful next question concerns the omitted interaction. [Invite one student to state that question in everyday language, then move to the decision.]

Delivery guidance · not spoken

Transition

Model only the timing distinction. Keep the Week 11 resolution protected.

Listen for

Premature solutions, database slogans, or claims that sequential tests are useless.

Rescue or cut

Say: “Today we identify the missing condition and the evidence we need. The later maintenance record will address the response.”

From testing to engineering review

A review turns evidence into a proportionate next decision

ReconstructTracePrioritiseExplain consequenceRecommendState quality implication

Review note

A review should improve the supplied proposal without replacing it. For an omission, explain which decision it prevents and what could happen if the team guesses. A missing heading alone is not a consequential issue.

Anchor · if you lose the thread

A review improves a supplied proposal.

Say · rehearsal script

In the second block, your task is to advise a team about the proposal they actually supplied. First reconstruct its responsibilities. Trace a design decision to a requirement, then prioritise issues by consequence before recommending a response.

An omission matters when it prevents a decision. Ask what could happen if the team guesses. A missing diagram title may leave the design understandable; an unspecified unit on a measurement may lead two readers to implement different behaviour. These are general examples, not clues to the unseen proposal. [Invite a distinction between cosmetic and consequential omissions.]

Evaluate a defensible choice as well as a weakness. Proportionate advice preserves what already serves the requirement. A replacement architecture usually prevents us from seeing whether you understood this one. [Reconnect to responsibility boundaries and traceability.]

Delivery guidance · not spoken

Transition

Reconnect the chain to the Week 6–9 practice. Emphasise that recommendation follows consequence. Use the practice guide to contrast one harmless omission with a consequential gap; do not use hidden assessment examples.

Listen for

Students planning an alternative architecture or listing every issue.

Rescue or cut

Ask: “What is the smallest response that reduces the consequence you just named?”
