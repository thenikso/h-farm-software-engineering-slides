Week 08 · LO4

If the fault disappears, did we debug it?

A changed symptom is not yet a supported cause.

Textbook study

Chapter 8 introduction, pp. 227-228, and §8.2, p. 244. Printed pages in Sommerville (2016), 10th edition, Global Edition. See the session 8 reading guide for the study question and optional selections.

Purpose

Debugging is a structured investigation that connects reproduction, observations, hypotheses, checks, cause, and verification.

Say · rehearsal script

A disappearing symptom is not yet an explanation. Someone changes something, the error stops appearing, and the team feels relieved. What would justify saying the cause has been understood?

Today we preserve the investigation path. You will record observations, competing explanations, checks, and the limits of the final claim. [Pause for one initial response.]

Explanation7 min

Structured investigation

Reproduce

Make the symptom observable.

Discriminate

Choose evidence that separates causes.

Verify

Test the fix and recurrence risk.

Not a straight line

Investigations loop as evidence changes what is plausible. The record should preserve decisions, not pretend the first guess was correct.

Say · rehearsal script

An investigation is a sequence of decisions. We reproduce or inspect the situation, separate observations from explanations, compare hypotheses, and choose checks that can change our confidence.

A fix and its verification come later. Moving straight to a change may remove the symptom while leaving us unable to explain it. [Invite students to locate their current step.]

Delivery guidance · not spoken

Teach aloud

Transition

Explanation8 min

Observation

What was seen, measured, logged, or reproduced.

Inference

What might explain those observations.

Keep both. Do not let one masquerade as the other.

Claim discipline

“The second attempt succeeded” is an observation. “The first request was lost” is one possible explanation.

Say · rehearsal script

Observation describes what was recorded. “Two messages arrived” is an observation. “The message service repeated delivery” is an explanation. Several explanations may fit the same observation.

Use language that keeps them separate. Read your first note and mark any causal claim you wrote as if it were a fact. [Give time to mark rather than erase it.]

Delivery guidance · not spoken

Check

Rescue

Reproduction record

Say · rehearsal script

Reproduction needs stated conditions. Which event, identity, action sequence, starting state, and visible outcome would you need to compare? A repeated attempt with changed conditions may answer a different question.

Do not claim a reproduction we have not performed. Our static record provides bounded observations; you can still specify the reproduction you would request. [Take one condition the class would preserve.]

Explanation7 min

A useful hypothesis

Explains the observations and predicts a difference

If this cause is present, then this check should show...

Competing explanations

Keep at least two plausible hypotheses long enough to choose evidence that can separate them.

Say · rehearsal script

Two hypotheses should make different predictions. For a neutral example, a page might show stale information or receive fresh but incorrect data. Checking what the server returned can separate those explanations.

Choose rivals that fit the current facts. You are not required to guess the final answer. You are required to show what evidence would change your view. [Return to the log.]

Delivery guidance · not spoken

Avoid

Explanation8 min

The best next check changes what you believe

Useful

Different hypotheses predict different results.

Weak

Every result can be explained after the fact.

Efficiency through discrimination

A check is valuable because it reduces uncertainty, not because it uses an impressive tool.

Say · rehearsal script

A useful check distinguishes explanations. Repeating a screen refresh may tell us little if both hypotheses predict the same outcome. Inspecting the right boundary can be more informative.

Write the expected difference before requesting the check. If result X occurs, what becomes weaker? If Y occurs, what would you investigate next? [Allow students to formulate predictions.]

Explanation8 min

A cause claim must explain the full observation set

Fits

Explains released facts.

Predicts

Anticipates a check result.

Excludes

Shows why a rival is weaker.

Root-cause reasoning

A supported cause is more than the last thing changed before the symptom disappeared.

Say · rehearsal script

A cause claim must fit the observation set. It should explain the released facts, predict something useful, and show why a rival account is less convincing. A neat story alone does not meet that standard.

Keep competing reports separate. A cause for Issue 184 need not explain Issue 191. We can make progress while retaining an open question. [Compare two live claims without announcing a mechanism.]
